Framework

What Ashtakoota Guna Milan measures

Ashtakoota is a Moon-based compatibility scoring system used in traditional North Indian marriage matching. It combines eight Kootas for a maximum of 36 points. Most of the inputs derive from the two people’s Moon signs and Nakshatras.

The score is a structured first layer, not a complete relationship verdict. The higher-weight Kootas can strongly affect the total, and traditional exceptions or cancellation rules may change how individual issues are judged.

Eight Kootas

The 36-point distribution

KootaMaximum pointsWhat it broadly represents
Varna1Traditional spiritual/temperamental classification
Vashya2Mutual influence/relationship category
Tara3Birth-star relationship based on Nakshatra counting
Yoni4Traditional instinctive/physical compatibility symbolism
Graha Maitri5Friendship relationship of Moon-sign lords
Gana6Temperamental grouping of the Nakshatras
Bhakoot7Relationship between the two Moon signs
Nadi8Nakshatra-based Nadi classification; traditionally high-weighted

Total maximum: 36 points. A total should always be read together with the individual Koota breakdown.

Quick vs Full Match

Why a fuller chart analysis may keep the same 36-point score

The core Ashtakoota score is fundamentally Moon/Nakshatra based. If a quick match already uses the correct Moon signs and Nakshatras, a full birth-detail calculation can confirm the same eight-Koota score rather than replace it. The value of the full mode is that it can add chart layers that are outside the 36 points.

Those extra layers may include Lagna, seventh-house factors, Mangal Dosha, Navamsa and Dasha timing. Therefore “same Guna score” does not mean the full calculation added nothing; it means the 36-point component remained the same while the surrounding assessment became richer.

Interpretation Discipline

Read the Koota breakdown before the total

Two couples can have the same total score through very different combinations of Kootas. Because the point weights range from 1 to 8, a total can hide where the agreement or disagreement is concentrated. Look at Nadi, Bhakoot, Gana, Graha Maitri and the other factors individually before discussing the total.

Traditional matching also contains exception rules and regional variations. A software score is therefore best used as transparent arithmetic and a structured checklist—not as an automated approval or rejection of two people.

Common Questions

Frequently asked questions

Is 18 out of 36 always enough for marriage?

A threshold is sometimes used as a screening convention, but no single total can responsibly decide a marriage. The Koota breakdown and wider charts matter.

Why does Nadi carry 8 points?

In the Ashtakoota weighting system Nadi is assigned the highest point value, which is why it can strongly change the total score.

Does Guna Milan check Mangal Dosha?

Not in the core 36-point score. Mangal Dosha is a separate chart-based check.

Can exact birth time improve matching?

Yes. Exact time supports Lagna, houses, Navamsa and other chart layers beyond Moon-based Ashtakoota.
