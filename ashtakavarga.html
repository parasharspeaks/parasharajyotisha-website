Definition

What Ashtakavarga is

Ashtakavarga is a point-based Jyotisha system that distributes supportive indications, commonly called bindus, across the twelve zodiac signs. It is used to add a structured strength layer to house and transit analysis.

The system is not the same as the eight Kootas of marriage matching. “Ashta” here refers to the contributors used in the point framework, while Ashtakoota is an entirely different compatibility system.

Two views

Bhinna Ashtakavarga and Sarvashtakavarga

BAV — Bhinna AshtakavargaA planet-specific point pattern across the twelve signs. It helps examine how a particular planet may fare when transiting different signs.
SAV — SarvashtakavargaA combined sign-wise total obtained from the relevant BAV contributions. It gives a broader comparative pattern across the zodiac/houses.
BindusHigher or lower points are comparative indicators inside the system, not stand-alone guarantees of good or bad events.
How to use it

A practical interpretation sequence

First read the natal chart normally: Lagna, houses, lords, planets, Yogas and Dasha. Then use Ashtakavarga to add quantitative context to the houses or signs involved. When a major transit is being studied, compare the transiting planet’s BAV and the relevant SAV pattern rather than relying on the sign transit alone.

Natal foundationDo not let a point total replace the actual birth-chart structure.
Dasha contextA transit is more meaningful when the relevant themes are active in the period system.
Transit contextSaturn and Jupiter transit interpretation often uses Ashtakavarga as an additional filter.
Relative readingPoint patterns are best compared within the same chart rather than converted into universal promises.
How the Pattern Is Built

Ashtakavarga uses fixed relative-position rules

For each relevant planet, classical Ashtakavarga rules evaluate specific relative positions from the planets and Lagna and assign supportive points to zodiac signs. The resulting planet-specific pattern is its Bhinna Ashtakavarga. Combining the relevant planetary patterns produces the broader Sarvashtakavarga view used to compare signs and houses.

The method is rule based rather than a subjective score invented after the event. At the same time, implementations can differ in presentation and in some detailed conventions, so it is useful to understand whether a program is showing raw BAV, SAV, house totals or a derived interpretation.

Transit Example

How Ashtakavarga adds context to Gochar

Suppose Saturn is transiting a particular sign. A basic transit reading identifies that sign relative to the natal chart. An Ashtakavarga layer then looks at Saturn’s relevant BAV support and the broader SAV strength of that sign/house. The point pattern can help distinguish comparatively more supported and less supported zones within the same chart.

This is still not a stand-alone event predictor. The natal house, its lord, Saturn’s natal role, the active Dasha and other transits remain part of the final synthesis. Ashtakavarga is valuable precisely because it adds another structured piece of evidence rather than replacing the rest of Jyotisha.

Common Questions

Frequently asked questions

Is Ashtakavarga the same as Ashtakoota?

No. Ashtakavarga is a planetary point system; Ashtakoota is the 36-point marriage matching framework.

What is BAV?

Bhinna Ashtakavarga is the planet-specific distribution of points across the twelve signs.

What is SAV?

Sarvashtakavarga is the combined sign-wise point total used for a broader comparative view.

Can a high SAV score guarantee success?

No. It is one strength indicator and must be read with the natal chart, Dasha and the specific transit being studied.
